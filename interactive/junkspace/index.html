Junkspace™

Some places are abandoned. Others were erased.

JUNKSPACE is a story-driven science-fiction adventure about a salvage contractor who finds evidence of a civilization deliberately removed from the record—and a Company that may know why.

In development at Rock River Interactive

Humanity spread across the stars a long time ago.

Colonies became cities. Shipping lanes became routine. Corporations built ports, moved cargo, stripped worlds, lost ships, filed reports and kept civilization running.

And somewhere along the way, an entire inhabited destination disappeared.

Not destroyed.

Erased

Its routes vanished from navigation systems. Its records disappeared from databases. Official histories stopped mentioning it.

As if millions of lives had simply never been there.

But not everything was deleted.

The Contract

You weren’t looking for a conspiracy.

You were looking for a paycheck.

In JUNKSPACE you work the forgotten edges of civilization as an independent salvage contractor aboard The Wayward.

The jobs aren’t glamorous.

  • Recover a navigation core from a dead freighter.
  • Restore power to an abandoned ship.
  • Inspect a damaged station.
  • Retrieve equipment.
  • Find out why something stopped working — and what’s still worth bringing home.

Then one routine recovery produces something that shouldn’t exist: a valid navigation route to a place that isn’t in the records anymore.

The deeper you dig, the less accidental the absence begins to look.

Someone removed this place from the galaxy. And the Company may know why.

Evidence

The records say one thing.
The ship says another.

Officially, ships suffer failures. Crews evacuate. Cargo gets abandoned. Contracts get closed. Life moves on.

But machinery keeps its own history.

  • A terminal that was accessed after the supposed evacuation.
  • A system deliberately isolated instead of damaged.
  • A crew log that doesn’t match the official report.
  • A navigation computer carrying coordinates that every modern database insists don’t exist.

Piece by piece, ordinary salvage jobs begin exposing a much larger story.

And the people paying you to recover the evidence may have reasons for wanting it back.

In-engine capture — work in progress

The World

This is a working future.

JUNKSPACE isn’t a universe of spotless starships and gleaming corridors. It’s a future built by people who had budgets.

Freighters are patched together. Industrial stations have service corridors behind the nice parts. Machines have breakers, access panels, maintenance routes and warning labels. Ports are crowded with workers, cargo, equipment and decades of accumulated infrastructure.

Things wear out.

Things get repaired.

Things get bypassed.

And sometimes things are deliberately made to stop working.

Understanding these places is part of playing the game.

In-engine capture — work in progress

Systems

Solve problems, not puzzles.

A locked door isn’t locked because the game wants you to hunt for the blue key.

It’s locked because something locked it.

  • Find the right crew credential.
  • Restore power and manipulate the security system.
  • Discover enough information to invoke legitimate salvage authority.
  • Find another way around.
  • Or cut through the damn thing.

JUNKSPACE is built around problems with multiple legitimate solutions. What you carry matters. What you’ve repaired matters. What you’ve discovered matters.

And sometimes what you know is more useful than what you own.

Consequence

The world remembers what you did.

JUNKSPACE doesn’t reduce your decisions to a dialogue option marked GOOD or BAD. Choices happen through action.

  • Repair somethingorbypass it
  • Preserve the shipordamage it
  • Search deeperorfinish the contract
  • Turn over what you foundorkeep digging
  • Take the clean routeortake the expensive one

The game remembers the actual state of the world: the systems you changed, the things you recovered, the information you learned and the damage you left behind.

There isn’t always a correct solution. There are consequences.

Environmental Storytelling

Every wreck has two stories.

There is the story in the report. And there is the story left behind.

In the report

  • Catastrophic system failure
  • Crew evacuated safely
  • Cargo written off
  • Contract closed
  • No further action required

Left behind

  • The shipHow its crew lived
  • Maintenance recordsWhat kept breaking
  • Power systemWhat was shut down
  • TerminalsWho was still aboard
  • NavigationWhere it went

And sometimes those stories don’t agree with the one you were given.

Home Base

The Wayward

Between contracts, you return to The Wayward.

Your ship.

Your workshop.

Your transport.

Your home.

What begins as a series of salvage jobs gradually becomes a journey across ports, derelicts, industrial installations and forgotten corners of inhabited space.

Every contract can pay the bills. Some can improve your ship. Some uncover pieces of the past.

And some lead back toward the same impossible question.

How do you erase a civilization?
